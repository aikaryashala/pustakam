Unit IV · Presentations with AI

17. Slides with Gemini AI

Gemini does not make a full presentation in one click like Gamma. Instead, it helps in two ways. First, it plans the content. Second, it works inside Google Slides to build it. This is Lab experiment 14.

Way 1: Gemini plans, you build

Step 1: Ask Gemini for the structure

prompt
I need a 10-slide presentation on "Rainwater harvesting for apartments in Visakhapatnam" for first year civil engineering students.
For each slide give: slide title, 3 to 4 bullet points (short), and one idea for an image.
Also give speaker notes of 2 to 3 sentences for each slide.

Gemini writes the whole plan. Read it. Ask for changes: "Make slide 4 about costs in rupees." "Add a slide with a simple diagram idea."

Step 2: Build the slides

Option A: Google Slides

  1. Open slides.google.com. Make a new presentation.
  2. Choose a theme.
  3. For each slide in Gemini's plan: add a slide, paste the title and bullets.
  4. Paste the speaker notes in the notes box under each slide.
  5. Add images (next step).

Option B: Gamma's "Paste in text". Paste Gemini's plan into Gamma. It makes the slides for you.

Option C: Ask Gemini: "Give me this presentation as a Markdown file." Then import it into Gamma or a tool like Marp.

Step 3: Add images

  • Ask Gemini: "Create an image of a rooftop rainwater harvesting system on an apartment, simple diagram style." Download and insert.
  • In Google Slides, click Insert → Image → Search the web.
  • Use Unsplash or Pexels for free photos.

Way 2: Gemini inside Google Slides

If your Google account has Gemini for Workspace (many college and some personal accounts do), there is a Gemini button at the top right of Google Slides.

  • Create a slide: Click Gemini → "Create a slide about ..." It adds a slide with text and an image.
  • Create an image: Click Gemini → "Create an image of ..." for the current slide.
  • Summarise: Ask Gemini to summarise the deck or suggest a better title.
  • Rewrite text: Select text and ask Gemini to make it shorter or clearer.

If you do not see the Gemini button, use Way 1. It works for everyone.

Tip: Use both. Plan with Gemini. Build with Gamma. Polish in Google Slides.

Try it

  • Ask Gemini for a 10-slide plan on a topic from your branch. Then build it in Google Slides.
  • Ask Gemini to write a script for what you will say on each slide. Practise it.
  • Put the plan into Gamma and compare the two decks.
  • Open slides.google.com and look for the Gemini button. If it is there, make one slide with it.

New words

  • Structure — the order and parts of something.
  • Speaker notes — notes under a slide that the presenter sees but the audience does not.
  • Deck — another word for a full set of slides.
  • Markdown — a simple way of writing formatted text using symbols like # and -.
  • Insert — to add something (an image, a slide) into a document.
  • Polish — to make the final small improvements.
  • Script — the words you will say while presenting.
  • Workspace — Google's set of office apps: Docs, Slides, Sheets, Gmail, Drive.
