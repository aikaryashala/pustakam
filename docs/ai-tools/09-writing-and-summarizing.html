Unit II · Prompt Engineering

9. Writing and summarizing with AI

AI tools are very good at two things: writing text and shortening text. This chapter shows both.

AI-assisted writing

The key word is assisted. AI helps you write. You still decide what to say. A good process has four steps.

  1. Plan. Decide the topic, the audience and the length. Ask AI for an outline.
  2. Draft. Ask AI to write the first version from the outline.
  3. Edit. Read it. Fix facts. Add your own examples. Remove things you do not like. Ask AI to change the tone or the length.
  4. Check. Read it once more. Does it sound like you? Is every fact true?

Example prompt for step 1:

prompt
I need to write a 300-word article on "Solar power on college rooftops" for my college magazine. Give me an outline with 4 sections and one key point for each.

Example prompt for step 2:

prompt
Now write the full article from this outline. Simple English, short sentences, for first year students. Mention that Visakhapatnam gets about 300 sunny days a year.

Example prompts for step 3:

  • "Make the second paragraph shorter."
  • "Change the tone to be more exciting."
  • "Add one line about cost savings in rupees."
  • "Check the grammar and list the changes you made."

Other writing jobs AI does well

  • Fixing grammar: "Correct the grammar in this text. Do not change the meaning."
  • Changing tone: "Rewrite this message to be more polite."
  • Expanding: "Expand these 3 bullet points into a paragraph."
  • Translating: "Translate this to Telugu."
  • Simplifying: "Rewrite this for a 12-year-old."

Summarization

A summary is a short version of a long text. It keeps only the main points. AI can summarise an article, a chapter, a research paper, a meeting or a YouTube video.

Paste the text (or upload the file) and ask:

prompt
Summarise this article in 5 bullet points. Then write a one-sentence summary. Keep the key numbers.

Types of summary you can ask for:

Ask forYou get
"TL;DR in one line"One sentence
"5 bullet points"Key points
"Executive summary"One short paragraph for a busy manager
"Summary for a 10-year-old"Very simple version
"Key terms with meanings"A glossary
"Questions this text answers"Good for exam revision

Tip: For long PDFs, use NotebookLM. It does not forget the start of the document, and it shows you where each point came from.

Be careful

  • A summary can miss something important. For exams, read the original once.
  • AI may add things that were not in the text. Ask: "Only use information from the text I gave."
  • Do not submit AI writing as your own creative work without editing it. Teachers can tell.

Try it

This is Lab experiment 6.

  • Ask ChatGPT or Gemini to write a 300-word article on "Why every engineer should learn to swim" (or any topic you like).
  • Then ask it to summarise that same article in 3 bullet points and in one sentence.
  • Try the same with a real article: copy any news article from The Hindu and summarise it.
  • Paste a YouTube link into Gemini and ask for a summary of the video.

New words

  • Assisted — helped; AI helps you, but you are in charge.
  • Draft — the first rough version of a piece of writing.
  • Edit — to change and improve a piece of writing.
  • Tone — the mood of the writing: polite, formal, exciting.
  • Summary — a short version with only the main points.
  • TL;DR — "too long; didn't read" — a one-line summary.
  • Executive summary — a very short summary for a busy decision-maker.
  • Glossary — a list of words with their meanings, like this box.
