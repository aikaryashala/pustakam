# Documents for a given context

In college and at work, you must write many formal documents. Notices, letters, reports, invitations, emails to teachers. AI can write all of these in seconds, **if you give it the context**.

## What is context?

**Context** is the background. Who is writing? To whom? Why? When? Where? What is the tone?

Without context, the AI writes a general template with blanks like [Your Name]. With context, it writes a finished document.

## The context checklist

Before you ask AI for a document, collect these:

1. **Type** of document: notice, letter, email, report, invitation, circular.
2. **From** whom: your name, roll number, class, department.
3. **To** whom: HOD, Principal, students, parents, a company.
4. **Purpose**: what is this document for?
5. **Details**: date, time, venue, names, numbers.
6. **Tone**: formal, friendly, urgent.
7. **Length**: half page, one page.

## Example 1: A notice

```prompt
Write a formal notice from the Student Council of GVPIHLR, Visakhapatnam.
Event: Blood donation camp with KGH Blood Bank.
Date: 15 March 2027, 9 am to 2 pm.
Venue: College auditorium, Madhurawada campus.
Who can join: All students and staff above 18 years.
Include: a line asking students to have breakfast before donating, and a contact (Secretary, Student Council, 98xxxxxxxx).
Format: Title, date, body, signature. Under 150 words.
```

## Example 2: An email to a teacher

```prompt
Write a short, polite email to my HOD, Dr. Rao, asking for permission to miss the lab on Friday because I am representing the college in a chess tournament in Hyderabad. My name is K. Priya, roll number 26CSE045, CSE section B. Offer to complete the lab later.
```

## Example 3: A report

```prompt
Write a one-page industrial visit report. Visit: Visakhapatnam Port, 3 February 2027. Group: 40 first year ECE students with 2 faculty. We saw: container handling, the control room, and a talk on automation. Sections: Introduction, What we saw, What we learned, Conclusion. Formal English. Third person.
```

## Example 4: An invitation

```prompt
Write an invitation card text for our department's technical fest "Tarang 2027". Date: 20 and 21 March 2027. Place: GVPIHLR campus, Visakhapatnam. Chief guest: a scientist from NSTL Visakhapatnam. Events: coding contest, robo race, paper presentation. Tone: warm and exciting. Under 80 words.
```

## Types of documents and what to remember

| Document | Key things to include |
|---|---|
| Notice | Title, date of issue, event details, who it is for, signature |
| Formal letter | Sender address, date, receiver address, subject, salutation, body, closing |
| Email | Clear subject line, short body, polite closing, your name and roll number |
| Report | Title, introduction, body with headings, conclusion, date and author |
| Invitation | Event name, date, time, place, chief guest, RSVP contact |
| Circular | Reference number, date, subject, instructions, authority |

## After AI writes it

- Read it fully. Fix any wrong details.
- Replace any [blanks] the AI left.
- Paste it into Google Docs or MS Word for formatting.
- Ask AI: *"Check this for spelling and grammar."* one last time.

## Try it

- Pick one: a notice, a letter, an email, a report or an invitation.
- Write down the context using the checklist above. Then write the prompt.
- Generate it. Then change one thing in the context (for example, the date) and ask AI to update only that.

## New words

- **Context** — the background information around a task.
- **Formal** — official and proper in style.
- **Notice** — a written announcement put up for many people to read.
- **Circular** — an official notice sent to many people in an organisation.
- **Venue** — the place where an event happens.
- **Salutation** — the greeting at the start of a letter: "Dear Sir".
- **RSVP** — a request to reply and say if you will come.
- **Third person** — writing with "the students", "they", not "I" or "we".
- **Template** — a ready-made pattern with blanks to fill in.
